# Judge: The Purist

## 1. Who I am

I came up in the Processing years: Tarbell's Substrate and Sand Traveler, Hodgin's Magnetosphere and flocking, Reas's Process series, Watz's Electroplastique, the OpenProcessing feed on a Friday night. I care whether an image comes out of a system, whether that system can still surprise its author, and whether minute ten is as good as minute one. I trust a scene less when I can see the illustration it started from.

I looked at all 72 contact sheets (Arcs from the re-sampled `arcs-offset` sheet at half-seconds) and every 96 s sheet that exists, and I read the technique and lineage notes of the scenes whose systems I wanted to understand.

## 3. My top 10

**1. Interference (9).** This is what I mean by a system. Two sheets of near-identical lines are multiplied like stacked transparencies, and everything you see (the great sweeping fringes, the folds, the bloom of rings) is a property of their difference, not of either sheet. The maker learned the right lesson in its lineage: only near-twins make legible fringes, so show pairs. The music never draws anything. The kick is written into a 1.6 s phase history and read at distance over speed, so one pixel of line movement becomes a fringe swinging across the stage: the smallest possible input amplified by the geometry itself. The 96 s sheet has a real arc, from straight currents to great arcs to rings and back, because the sheet centres travel logarithmically in from 20,000 units away. It is black and white, it has no glow, and it would sit comfortably next to Bridget Riley or Molnár. It is also the only batch-01 piece Raph could read as music, which tells you a system and legibility are not enemies.

**2. Stamps (9).** The quietest masterpiece in the collection. Cubes roll by rigid rotation about their leading edge, and whatever face lands prints its motif, through the landing orientation, with misregistration and paper tooth, into a persistent floor texture that slowly re-papers itself. The image is the record of the process, as with Tarbell's Substrate or Reas's Process series. You can watch the carpet go from a few scattered prints at 2 s to a dense, patterned textile by the drop, and at 96 s it is different again because the record has faded and been overwritten. Cheerful four-ink palette, daylight, long real shadows, no glow. This scene is the collection's best answer to TASTE.md's call for range, and it got there through a rule rather than a style.

**3. Current (8).** A flow field is the most worn technique in generative art, and Current survives it because the maker broke it on purpose: mixing potential gradient into the curl so the field is no longer divergence-free, which makes drains, voids and braided rivers instead of Hodgin's even grey silk. The ink buffer is Float32 because 8-bit alpha cannot hold a 1% deposit (a detail the old guard learned the hard way), and the single-hue LUT keeps the palette honest. At 96 s it neither saturates nor dies. The drop is visibly more tangled and the breakdown lets the strands lengthen. It could be louder about the kick, but it is the purest piece of flow-field drawing in the set.

**4. Chladni (8).** Real physics you can watch think. Tens of thousands of grains descend the gradient of f² on a sum of true plate modes, with random kicks scaled by amplitude, so when the snare changes the mode the whole plate of sand visibly migrates to the new nodal figure rather than cutting to it. That migration, the in-between state where sand is still looking for its lines, is the most beautiful thing in the scene, and it is pure emergence. The raking two-colour light turns density into relief. My one complaint is the murky purple ground, which a black-glass or matte slate would serve better.

**5. Physarum (8).** Jones's 2010 agent model, run faithfully on the GPU in two species. The music drives a parameter (the sensor angle) rather than a picture, so the network itself changes character from fine lace to coarse cells, which is exactly the right place for bass. Food nodes on the kick are reached for, so the kick produces behaviour, not a flash. The quarter-res bloom is too generous and fogs the veins; slime mould looks best as sharp filaments on black.

**6. Chomper (8).** The biggest surprise in the set. A real self-playing maze game (BFS chomper, four pursuers with their own targeting rules) leaves flowers in the corridors it has cleared, so the board is a memory of play. Then in the drop the whole board is folded, in the vertex shader, onto a torus, a drum and a tunnel. That is a topological transformation of a live system, not an effect laid on top of a picture. At 96 s the palettes and topologies differ and the flower trail keeps accumulating. It is playful in a way generative art too rarely allows itself.

**7. Coral (8).** Gray-Scott is a textbook form, and the textbook look is uniform tubes. Coral's departure is a spatially varying feed/kill map, so labyrinth, spots and dividing cells coexist and their borders migrate on a slow current. That is what reefs actually look like. The 96 s sheet never repeats. The kicks are too subtle (seeded blots bloom slowly), but a reaction–diffusion system should be slow, and I would rather it stay honest than pulse.

**8. Attractor (8).** A strange attractor as long-exposure photography, colour from the age of the light, and a running Lyapunov estimate that stops the coefficients wandering into dead orbits. That guard is precisely the kind of algorithmic care this field needs. The forms morph continuously and the 96 s sheet shows genuinely different bodies (horns, tori, veils). It loses a point because Clifford and de Jong maps have been a first-week exercise since Paul Bourke. It is excellent, and it is still a quotation.

**9. Knit (7).** The fabric grows. Rows are stitched one at a time, each band charted procedurally, and what has been knitted stays. At 96 s you are looking at a genuine history of the set, red snowflakes over brown houses over blue stars. The window shadow crossing the work is a quiet, real light. It is the best proof in the collection that a textile, a medium defined by rules, can carry music as accumulation rather than pulse.

**10. Zen (7).** The rake writes the height field. Patterns (grids, spirals, checkerboards) are not swapped in: you watch the tool draw them, and the kick rings the stones. It is the Reas idea that the process should be visible in the output, in the gentlest possible register. Low sun, real shadows, matte gravel. At 96 s the garden has been re-raked several times and looks lived in.

Near misses: Scanlines, Iso City, Mobile and Thresholds all sit at 7 and could replace Zen on a different day.

## 4. Weakest 8

These are the harness pieces. The 2016 originals are low on my scale too, but they are sketches by one person learning, and they are the seeds of the best work here, so I discuss them under Themes rather than here.

**Magazine Cover (1).** A flat illustrated rooftop party whose 96 s sheet is eight copies of one picture. Nothing in it could produce an image its author did not draw. *Fix:* keep the rooftop, but make the crowd a system. Give each dancer a small body grammar (limb angles from a few oscillators, phase-locked to the beat with individual lag) and let the crowd self-organise: spacing by separation forces, clusters forming around the DJ in the drop and loosening in the breakdown. The string lights could be a hanging-chain simulation that sways with the bass. Then the cover is a place that behaves.

**Diner (2).** A neon sign and its reflection. *Fix:* make neon the system. A neon tube is a path with a gas discharge, so build the sign from tubes that ignite along their length at a speed set by the music, flicker with a real failure model (a starter that misfires, a tube that dies and restarts), and let the hats buzz the ballast. Better still, let a generative script-lettering system write new signs from a word list, so the diner isn't the same diner at minute five.

**Mass Poster (2).** Two silhouettes high-five on every beat forever. *Fix:* the sunburst is the only generative element; promote it. Make the crowd at the bottom thousands of tiny figures whose arms rise as a travelling wave (a stadium wave, cellular-automaton style, seeded by the kick) and let the slogan be printed by that crowd, card stunt style, with each figure holding one pixel of the word. That is mass as a system rather than mass as a subject.

**Ophanim (2).** A centred emblem turning forever, which TASTE.md's own first "falls flat" item describes. *Fix:* the gimbal rings are the interesting part. Let the rings be a genuine coupled system (each ring's rotation axis attached to the previous ring's frame, like a real gyroscope nest or a double pendulum) so their combined motion is chaotic and never repeats, and let the eyes be distributed by a relaxation rule on each ring's surface. Drop the wings, or make them feathers laid down by a growth rule.

**Low Poly (2).** The triangulated-poster look peaked in 2014. *Fix:* use triangulation for what it is good at, which is being a live mesh. Build a Delaunay mesh whose points are particles in a flow field; the whale is not a model but a density of points that the flow gathers and disperses. Colour each triangle by sampling a slowly moving gradient field at its centroid. The kick adds points; the breakdown removes them, so the image coarsens and refines with the music.

**WPA (2).** A national-park poster whose taglines change. *Fix:* the WPA silkscreens were layered flat inks, so generate the landscape: ridgelines from 1D noise at several octaves, each a separate ink layer with slight misregistration, the lake reflecting them, and new landscapes arriving via a slow screen-wipe as each "print" is pulled. Let the fireworks be proper particle bursts clipped to one ink.

**Expressway (3).** Synthwave is a costume. *Fix:* keep the drive but let the city be procedural at the level of rules: road network by L-system or tensor-field streamlines, buildings extruded from the parcels that network leaves. The drop does not turn it into a neon tunnel; it lengthens the city's blocks and raises the towers, as Iso City does, so what you see is the city growing to meet you.

**Rave (3).** A picture of the thing it accompanies. *Fix:* invert it. Remove the silhouettes and keep only the smoke and the beams, and make the smoke a real advected density field (the one Current already has) that the lasers reveal. The music then shapes the medium, not the drawing, and the room sees its own lasers in a field that remembers where they have been.

## 5. Themes

**1. The simulations won.** Every one of my top eight is a simulation or a rule set (flow, reaction–diffusion, agents, grain physics, interference, a game, a rolling rigid body). The harness's own critics reached the same conclusion from a different direction: Interference, Current and Coral were the batch-01 winners. The lesson is not "make simulations"; it is that the music should drive a parameter of a process, not trigger a picture. Physarum's sensor angle, Chladni's mode number and Interference's phase history are the three best musical mappings in the collection, and none of them draws anything directly.

**2. Batch 05 and some of batch 04 is illustration with a beat.** Poster styles (WPA, Mass Poster, Magazine Cover, Diner, Low Poly, Vorticism, Penny Dreadful, Republic, Constructivist) share a pattern: one authored composition, a title that cycles, one "drop state". Their 96 s sheets are almost identical to their 24 s ones. This is the harness following TASTE.md's call for range (daylight, print, flat colour) with the most literal reading available: *depict* a print. The better pieces in that batch found the process inside the medium instead: Letterpress (the press as a timeline), Stamps (the print as a record), Knit (the stitch as a unit), Futurist (words as particles). Range should come from media processes, not media looks.

**3. Accumulation is the collection's secret weapon.** Stamps, Knit, Chomper's flowers, Futurist's word piles, Current's ink buffer, Zen's raked gravel and Iso City's growing towers all keep what the music did. These are the scenes whose 96 s sheets are more interesting than their 24 s ones, which is the purist's test of longevity. Scenes without memory (the posters, Diner, Ophanim) are the same picture at minute ten. Almost every scene could gain from a layer that remembers.

**4. Two-state toggles.** Stargate (blue/gold), Nebula (red/teal), Expressway, Aurora, Paper and Terminal each have a calm state and a drop state and interpolate between them. It reads well on a 24 s sheet and badly on a 96 s one, where every drop is the same drop. The good scenes vary *continuously* with the music (Physarum's sensor angle, Chladni's mode number) or carry a slow independent clock (Interference's 90 s centre drift, Iso City's day-night cycle). Every scene needs a clock slower than the song.

**5. The harness makes craft cheap and ideas scarce.** The technical level is remarkable: CRT beam models, fwidth-hatched engraving, rigid-body rolling, Lyapunov guards. What the process tends to produce, though, is a high-fidelity rendering of the brief's noun: a jellyfish, a diner, an angel. Briefs that named a technique and demanded a departure from it (LOOP.md's lesson from batch 01) produced the systems; briefs that named a style or a subject produced illustrations. The critic loop rewards legibility, which pushes toward subjects people can name. I would add a rubric line: "could this image have surprised its maker?"

**6. Centred emblems persist.** Iris, Ophanim, Text, Rings, the Arcs default and Cube all put one object in the middle. TASTE.md rejects the centred pulsing shape, yet it keeps coming back in costume. The systems scenes are all-over or composed across the frame: fields, carpets, networks.

**7. Raph's 2016 originals are the collection's grammar.** Arcs (phase-staggered sweeps on a grid), Rings (segmented concentric rings), Parametric (string art between curves), Jags (polylines driven by bands), Flyover (spectrum as terrain) and Planets (orbits with trails) are simple and often under-staged at their defaults. Arcs is a single crescent unless you open the grid; Parametric is mostly black. But their grammar reappears, grown up, in Orphism (Rings and Arcs), Scanlines (Flyover), Magnetosphere (Planets) and Interference (the line-sheet sensibility of Jags). They were honest sketches: each is one rule, openly exposed to the music. The fix they need is only staging: defaults that fill the stage (Arcs at a 6×6 grid with staggers on, Parametric with a longer trail and thicker lines), which would make them fine minimalist pieces.

**8. Glow is still the default finish.** Physarum, Mandala, Abyss, Bloom and Magnetosphere all lose definition to bloom. The strongest images (Interference, Stamps, Thresholds, Chladni's raking light, Mobile's shadows) use contrast, line and shadow. TASTE.md already says this. The systems scenes would benefit most from obeying it, because their structure is fine and bloom erases it.

## 6. Transplants

1. **Kick-as-phase-echo from Interference → Coral, Scanlines, Chladni, Op Art.** The kick is stored as a history and read at distance divided by speed, so it travels as a wavefront through the system rather than landing everywhere at once. In Coral it would become a ring of changed feed rate expanding through the reef; in Scanlines, a phase ripple across the raster; in Op Art, a travelling warp through the checks. It is the most elegant "visible but confined" kick in the collection.

2. **Persistent world-space record from Stamps → Koi, Zen, Murmuration, Skyline.** Stamps keeps a torus texture of the floor that takes every print and slowly re-papers. Koi's pond floor could keep the silt trails the fish leave; Murmuration's sky could keep a faint long exposure of the flock's path, as in the 19th-century photographs of starlings; Skyline's smoke could accumulate into a real haze that the fireworks then light.

3. **Music drives a parameter, not an event (Physarum's sensor angle) → Current, Attractor, Magnetosphere.** Current already has bass on turbulence. Let the centroid set the ratio of curl to gradient, so the field changes character between silk and drains with timbre. Attractor could map band energy onto one coefficient axis with the Lyapunov guard as the safety rail.

4. **The Lyapunov guard from Attractor → Magnetosphere, Descent, any chaotic system.** A running measure of whether the system has gone dead or exploded, used to steer parameters back to the interesting region. Magnetosphere tends to collapse into the same three-ring rosette. A diversity measure, such as the spatial entropy of the trail buffer, could nudge the poles apart when it drops.

5. **Topology change as the drop, from Chomper → Chladni, Knit, Coral.** Folding a live 2D system onto a torus or drum in the vertex shader is a drop that transforms without flashing. Knit's fabric could roll into a tube as a sleeve; Coral's dish could wrap onto a sphere so the reef becomes a planet, with the simulation continuing uninterrupted underneath.

6. **Tool-writes-the-field from Zen → Sumi, Impasto, Letterpress.** In Zen you watch the rake make the pattern. Sumi's washes could be laid by a visible brush whose loading depletes; Impasto's strokes could be laid live by a brush agent following the flow field, so the painting is always half-finished and the drop speeds the painter up.

7. **Grain-to-figure migration from Chladni → Suprematism, De Stijl, Futurist.** Instead of tweening between authored compositions, let the elements descend a potential whose minima define the composition, and let the snare change the potential. Suprematist forms would then find their places through physics, jostling and settling, and no two arrivals would be the same.

8. **Slow independent clock from Interference (90 s centre drift) and Iso City (day-night) → Stargate, Nebula, Aurora, Abyss.** Each of these toggles between two states. Give them a clock slower than the song, such as corridor geometry or nebula chemistry drifting over 2–3 minutes, so every drop arrives in a different world.

9. **Float32 accumulation with a LUT from Current → Physarum, Magnetosphere, Mandala.** Current's high-dynamic-range ink buffer and single-hue tone curve do what bloom tries to do, without losing edges. Physarum's veins through a single-hue LUT, with bloom removed, would look like the Jones papers' best plates.

10. **Balanced physical hierarchy from Mobile → Ophanim, Orphism.** Mobile's tree of damped oscillators is genuinely physical and never repeats. Ophanim's rings and Orphism's discs could hang from the same kind of tree, so their rotations are coupled and emergent rather than scripted.

## 7. New ideas

**1. Substrate City.** *Lineage:* Tarbell's Substrate, with Iso City's growth and Stamps' persistent record. Crack-walkers start perpendicular to existing cracks and stop when they hit one, laying down a city plan of fine black lines on cream paper, each crack shadowed by a narrow band of sand-painted colour sampled from a small palette. *Music:* the kick spawns a new crack at the most recently completed intersection, so the city grows outward from where the beat last landed. Hats thicken the sand grain on the newest cracks. The drop raises the spawn rate and the breakdown lets it stall, so the long exposure sits there to be read. When the plate is full it re-papers cell by cell (the Stamps technique) and a new city begins. Minute ten is a different city from minute one.

**2. Differential Growth.** *Lineage:* Coral's morphology and Knit's accumulation, after Anders Hoff's inconvergent work. A single closed curve whose nodes repel each other, attract their neighbours, and split when an edge grows too long, so it folds into brain-coral and lettuce-leaf forms. Drawn as one continuous ink line on paper, previous states left as fading contour rings like tree growth. *Music:* the bass sets the growth rate, the snare inserts a burst of nodes at one point (a bud), the hats jitter the repulsion radius to fray the edge. The drop is a visible frenzy of folding; the breakdown lets the line relax and smooth.

**3. Space Colonisation Canopy.** *Lineage:* Physarum's reaching for food, Bloom's garden, the Runions algorithm. Attractor points are scattered in a crown shape, and a tree grows toward them by the space-colonisation rule, branch thickness by pipe-model. Drawn in ink with Mobile's cast shadows on a wall. *Music:* each kick scatters a small cluster of new attractors in one place, and you watch the nearest branches turn toward it over the next bars. The drop scatters many at once, so the tree lunges. In the breakdown, leaves drop along the branches in the order they grew. The tree is different every time it is played.

**4. Wave Function Collapse Tapestry.** *Lineage:* Knit's charted bands, Stamps' tiled motifs, Chomper's maze. A WFC solver fills the frame with tiles from a small adjacency set (Islamic-geometry or Fair Isle), collapsing cell by cell along an entropy frontier that visibly advances. *Music:* the kick collapses the next lowest-entropy cell (so the tapestry is woven at tempo), the snare swaps the tile set for the next region, the drop opens several frontiers at once. Contradictions, which WFC produces, are rendered as a brief frayed hole that re-weaves. This is the harness's layout pieces done as a genuine constraint system.

**5. Moiré Weave.** *Lineage:* Interference, Op Art, and Raph's Jags. Replace Interference's circles with two sheets of Jags-style polylines driven by band amplitudes, one sheet from the left channel of the spectrum and one from the right, or one a beat behind the other. The moiré is then literally the difference between the music now and the music a beat ago. Black on cream, a single vermilion thread where the fringes are steepest. It is the most direct marriage of Raph's 2016 grammar and the collection's best piece.

**6. Sand Traveler Orbits.** *Lineage:* Planets, Magnetosphere, Tarbell's Sand Traveler. The orrery is kept, but the planets are never drawn. Instead, between each pair of bodies, thousands of grains are sprinkled along the connecting line with a density falling off from each end, so as they orbit, the relations between planets paint soft, grainy sand nebulae onto an accumulating paper. *Music:* the bass widens the grain scatter, the kick adds a moon to one planet (a new relation to paint), the snare changes one orbit's eccentricity. The finished image at minute five is a portrait of the set's structure.

**7. Cellular Carpet.** *Lineage:* Stamps and Knit, Wolfram's elementary automata, 1990s Processing CA sketches. A 1D cellular automaton writes one row per beat down a long woven strip that scrolls away from the viewer in perspective like a runner carpet, each cell a stitch. *Music:* the rule number is chosen by the spectral centroid from a curated set of class-3 and class-4 rules (30, 45, 110, 150...), the kick injects a single flipped cell whose light cone spreads down the following rows, the drop switches to a two-dimensional rule for eight bars so the carpet blooms, the breakdown drops to a quiet class-2 rule. The strip is the song's score.

**8. DLA Frost.** *Lineage:* Coral's slow growth and Chladni's grains, after Witten and Sander. Diffusion-limited aggregation: random walkers stick to a growing crystal, drawn as frost on a dark window, lit by a streetlight that Mobile's shadow technique could cast. *Music:* the bass sets walker temperature (hot and fast in the drop, which gives dense dendrites; slow and cold in the breakdown, which gives spindly branches), each kick releases a burst of walkers from one edge so frost fronts creep in from different sides. Hats sparkle on the newest tips. Occasionally a warm breath (a long pad note) melts a patch, and the growth begins again.

**9. Plotter.** *Lineage:* Thresholds' line engraving, Zen's visible tool, Scanlines' raster. A simulated pen plotter draws, in real time and in one continuous path, a TSP-art or stipple rendering of a generative field (Current's flow density, say), with ink pooling where the pen slows. Pen colour changes on the snare as a pen swap; the kick makes the pen lift and jump to a new region. The image is always being drawn, the pen is always visible, and at the end of the song you have a drawing the room watched being made.
