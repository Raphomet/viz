My V1 scores are the ones from `harness/review/judge-purist.md`; all 24 scenes had one, so none is marked new. I looked at every V2 contact sheet and 96 s record (montaged frames for Koi and Physarum, which have no sheet) against its V1, opened full frames where the sheet was ambiguous (Chladni, Sumi, Koi, Interference, Zen, Low Poly, Rings, Attractor), and read the redesign notes only after scoring.

Mean V1 5.3 → V2 6.8. Twenty scenes improve, none gets worse, and four stay the same.

## Patterns

The biggest gains are all at the bottom, and they came from the same move: the redesigner found the rule already latent in Raph's 2016 sketch and gave it density and memory instead of decoration. Arcs, Parametric, Jags and Rings each gained three or four points without adding a single new technique. Those four are now among the most honest pieces in the collection, and they prove the originals were systems waiting for staging, not juvenilia. The poster pieces from the bottom (Vorticism, Low Poly, Grunge, Dot Matrix) also improved, by one or two points, because each was handed one rule (shearing slices, a refining mesh, spectrum-chosen letters). A rule grafted onto an illustration still reads as an illustration with a rule. At the top, the precise, subtractive edits won: removing bloom (Physarum), removing a sheen (Chladni), removing iridescence (Murmuration), removing a glow (Sumi). Each let the system show through. The top-12 redesigns that *added* a second event or layer (Current's dye, Zen's crew, Chomper's portal, Koi's rain) came out the same: sensible ideas that traded something V1 had for something new.

## Raph's question

**Yes, tangibly.** Twenty of the twenty-four are better on screen and none is worse. The improvement is largest exactly where the systems were buried: the old sketches.

- **Most want projected at a party: Physarum V2.** Sharp teal lace on black that visibly reaches for every kick. In the drop it stops exploring and commits to roads between what it has found. The room sees the music, and I see Jones and Tero. Parametric Lines on paper is my close second.
- **Would send back: Zen V2.** It has the right idea and the wrong material. The deep stripy grooves and the hard rectangular seams between raked patches turned a quiet combed garden into op-art with tiling artefacts. Keep the crew of rakes and restore V1's fine, shallow combing.

## What the redesigns lost

- **Interference:** the fine silver grain. V1 was a whisper that asked you to lean in; V2 is bolder and clearer, and more Riley than Molnár.
- **Current:** single-hue purity, and the drop's full-field braiding. The dye tracer is honest, but the drop now reads as the intro plus colour.
- **Zen:** the fine combed texture and its calm.
- **Twinkle Toph:** the intimacy of a daylight photograph of a friend, traded for a starfield set piece and a borrowed lightning bolt.
- **Flyover:** the raw white wireframe's candour about being data. The canyon is lovelier and less frank.
- **Sumi (silk look only):** the optional night rendering reads as a photographic negative of ink. The paper default is the piece; I would not demote it.
