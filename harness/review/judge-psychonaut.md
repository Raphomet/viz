# Judge: The Psychonaut

## 1. Who I am

I judge by Raph's own bar: is this extremely hard to look away from for someone who is very high? That means I reward whatever gives an altered mind somewhere to fall into (recursion, flight, breathing forms, detail inside detail, colour that keeps finding new chords), and I mark down anything that asks the viewer to read, flinch, decode a joke or sit through a white-out.

## 3. My top 10

**1. Mandala (9).** This is the scene I'd leave running all night. Every frame's mandala flies outward and ages from warm to cool, so the screen always holds several generations of the same thought at different distances. That is exactly how recursion feels from inside. At 14 s the orange core, green star and violet petal-veils in a thin thread of feedback look like something seen behind closed eyes. The kick stamps a new mandala into the centre and you *watch it leave*, which is the perfect confined beat. One reservation: the 96 s sheet toggles between a violet-white "drop" state and a rose-ember "intro" state, so by minute four the drop is predictable.

**2. Stargate (9).** The body leans forward. The slit-scan walls converge on a warm gate, stars race outward, and the corridor banks, so the vanishing point wanders and the flight feels steered rather than scripted. The drop opening from two walls to a four-way gold-and-rose cross (the 14 s full frame) is the best "more layers switch on" moment in the set. The breakdown's cool blue-violet is a proper exhale. Same caveat as Mandala: the drop is always gold-rose and the intro always blue.

**3. Oil (9).** The 96 s sheet is the proof. Cyan, magenta and yellow at 12 s; green, blue and amber at 24; blue, yellow and green at 36; teal, purple and lime at 48. It never lands on the same chord twice, and that is "colour that keeps finding new harmonies" done literally. The thin-film rims and trapped bubbles give the eye detail at two scales, the rotating dish carries colour across the frame, and the thumb-press kick is local and tactile. It's also the one psychedelic scene that earns its saturation, because the saturation is *historical* (the 1960s light show).

**4. Attractor (8).** A long-exposure photograph of a mathematical creature that never stops changing species. The 96 s sheet goes from infinity-loop silk to a lit torus to a bird-wing fold, all without a cut. Colouring by light-age (white leading edges, cool blue afterglow) makes motion itself visible in a still. Its one lack is a world: it floats on flat black with no depth cue. Give it motes or a far haze and it's a 9.

**5. Scanlines (8).** A glowing Rutt-Etra raster flown like a landscape, with a scanned planet rising. The quiet genius is the hidden image: over 96 s the terrain becomes a pond, egg-crate dunes and mountains, and the palette moves through green, amber, pink and ice blue. Kick rings pooling as bright ripples in the terrain at 12 and 60 s are a beautifully confined beat. The ice-blue dunes in the breakdown are the single calmest frame in the collection.

**6. Aurora (8).** Scale does the work here: the curtains fill the upper frame like a cathedral, and the still lake doubles them. Meteors and kick-lit spots on a curtain's lower edge put the beat *in* the sky rather than on it. It loses a point for being two-state at 96 s (bright green-and-purple drop, dim blue intro), and because the white-hot kick spot at 10–12 s briefly clips.

**7. Abyss (8).** Three depths of marine snow, god rays, and jellies whose tentacles pick up iridescent comb light: this is depth and gentleness at once, the most *kind* scene in the collection. The drop's gold halo rings around one jelly are a good confined kick. But the 96 s drops at 12, 60 and 84 s blow entire bells to white-hot glare. That's the one thing a sensitive eye will flinch at, and it's fixable.

**8. Nebula (8).** A volumetric flight into gas, with the soft sense of being inside a body. Nothing in it is sharp, and that is the point. Star flares are the right size for a kick. At 96 s it swings between a red-violet state and a teal-blue state, so as with the other two-state scenes, give it a slow third and fourth palette.

**9. Thresholds (8).** The only scene that does true visual recursion in a restrained, two-ink, engraved style: a door inside a porthole inside a gear inside a door. The drop inverting from dark to light ground at 12–16 s is a bold section change that still reads as the same world. The line density occasionally buzzes, and the gears flirt with Terry Gilliam machinery, but "falling through thresholds" is a perfect psychedelic premise done without a single rainbow. That makes it the strongest answer to the "range, not a house style" note.

**10. Chladni (7).** Sand snapping from one sacred geometry to the next is symmetry that shifts, the classic trip mechanic, and the snare-driven mode change is a legible and *beautiful* musical event. Amber-and-cyan raking light on violet cells is a chosen palette. The 96 s breakdown at 96 s (a fine star-lattice) versus the intro at 48 s (big quatrefoils) shows the scale moving with the music. It needs one more layer of depth (a camera drift or a vignette of the plate's edge) to become a 9.

(Just outside: Iris, Koi, Current, Physarum, Impasto, Magnetosphere, Murmuration, Sumi.)

## 4. Weakest 8

**Glitch (1).** Chromatic tearing, datamosh blocks and jittering type are the precise textures that feel bad when you're high: they read as your own perception breaking. *Fix:* keep the red sun and the cream paper, and drop the words and the tearing. Make the "glitch" a slow slit-scan smear, where the sun is drawn in horizontal bands that lag behind each other and ease back into register on the kick. That gives you distortion without jitter.

**Republic (1).** Hundreds of tiny glyphs, icons and tickers in acid yellow. A sober designer admires it. A high viewer drowns in it. *Fix:* take the Designers Republic *palette and geometry* (acid yellow, black, a single bold circular logo) and make one huge logo-mandala rotate slowly at the centre, with the micro-text reduced to a single ticker at the edge that moves at walking speed. Kick: one icon cell lights. Snare: the logo turns a notch.

**Twinkle Toph (1).** A halftone face on a white field, staring. A full-white projector field is a floodlight in a dark room, and a static face that watches you is unsettling. *Fix:* invert to dark ground, and let the halftone dots drift away from the face and re-form into it on the downbeat of each phrase, so it becomes a face-in-the-stars that you catch rather than one that catches you.

**Arcs (1) / Text (1).** Arcs, judged on the x.5 s offset sheet, is a single small white crescent sweeping at the centre of a black frame, and it reads as a loading spinner. Text draws a logo. *Fix:* retire them from the menu, or rebuild Arcs as a layer inside another scene (concentric arc-gates in Stargate's gate, or arcs in Orphism's discs) rather than as a scene.

**Penny Dreadful (2).** A skeletal ghost that floods blood-red at the drop is the one moment in the collection I would call menacing, and it sits inside a newspaper full of body copy. *Fix:* keep the woodcut town and moon, and lose the columns of text and the skull. Turn the fiddler into a moonlit dancer whose cloak streams in woodcut lines, and make the drop warm gold lamplight spreading window by window rather than red.

**Pop (2).** Five panels, SFX lettering and halftone, all fighting for attention at once. *Fix:* one panel, full-bleed. Push into the Ben-Day dots until each dot is a coloured world (a Droste zoom into halftone), with the kick as a single "POW" starburst that blooms from one dot and fades.

**Direct Address (2).** The message is lovely, but every scene that is mostly words asks a high brain to read, and reading ends trance. *Fix:* keep one phrase per section, set huge and slow, and let the word-rails become texture (unreadably small, blurred into horizontal light streaks) so they're felt rather than parsed.

Near-miss worth naming, because its failure is instructive: **Descent (5)** has the best premise in the set (a fractal tunnel of spheres within spheres) but ships at low resolution in a single muddy crimson. At 12 s the lower-left is a blown white glob and a pastel streak cuts across the frame. *Fix:* halve the kick's light, lift the base resolution, and give it Oil's rotating triads instead of one hue. Then it becomes the scene Mandala is to Stargate: the recursion scene *with walls*.

## 5. Themes

1. **Tunnels and recursion are the collection's strongest instrument, and it barely uses them.** Mandala, Stargate, Thresholds, Nebula and Scanlines are all forward motion into a vanishing point, and all score 8–9. Descent, Iris's Droste pupil, Terminal and Expressway's drop reach for it and fumble. Six of 72 is not enough for a bar written for altered minds.

2. **Words are trance-killers.** Batches 4 and 5 answered "range, not a house style" with about twenty graphic-design posters, and nearly all of them put type in the middle of the stage. For someone high, text engages exactly the mind they came to rest. The range scenes that *work* do range through **material** (paint in Impasto, water in Koi, ink in Sumi, sand in Chladni and Zen, engraving in Thresholds, paper in Paper, shadow in Mobile), not through typography. The lesson is "matte material, no words", not "posters".

3. **Two-state longevity.** The 96 s sheets expose something the 24 s sheets hide. Mandala, Stargate, Aurora, Nebula and Iris each have one drop look and one intro look, and they alternate. By minute five the drop has been seen. The scenes that truly unfold (Oil's colour chords, Attractor's forms, Scanlines' hidden images, Chladni's modes) are the ones I'd still be watching.

4. **The confined kick still clips to white.** Batch 2's "too pulsey" fix moved the kick into one place, but in several scenes that one place now saturates: Abyss's bells at 12/60/84 s, Descent's lower-left glob, Aurora's hot spot. A white blow-out on a projector is a flashbulb, even when it's local. The best kicks *travel*: Koi's ripples, Mandala's stamp flying outward, Scanlines' terrain rings, Skyline's single rising shell.

5. **Eyes and faces cut both ways.** Iris and Ophanim are among the most arresting images here, *because* eyes seize attention. But being stared at is the edge of paranoia, and Penny Dreadful's skull and Toph's face tip over. Clay's googly blobs show the friendly version: faces-in-things that are delighted, not watching. Rule of thumb: eyes that look *away*, blink, or are windows (Iris's pupil as a portal) are safe; eyes that fix on the viewer are not.

6. **The abstract simulations float, and the figurative scenes travel.** Attractor, Current, Coral, Physarum, Magnetosphere and Chladni are all flat fields on black with no camera. Aurora, Abyss, Scanlines, Impasto and Night Train move through a world. The best future scenes marry them: a simulation you fly *through*.

7. **Restraint beats spectrum.** Current (single cyan), Attractor (champagne and blue), Thresholds (two inks), Sumi (ink plus one red) and Coral (salmon and teal) all prove that one or two hues, deeply toned, hold a gaze as well as a rainbow. The rainbow moments that fail are the cheap ones: Murmuration's gradient flock and Expressway's prism tunnel.

8. **The original batch is from another era.** Text, Arcs, Rings, Jags, Parametric, Dot Matrix and Planets are single-effect sketches. They drag the menu down and should become layers or presets inside richer scenes, not stand-alone choices.

## 6. Transplants

1. **Oil's rotating colour triads → Mandala, Stargate, Nebula, Aurora, Iris**, because these five alternate between two fixed palettes, and Oil shows that giving every section a fresh three-colour chord (never the same twice in 96 s) is what keeps minute five as good as minute one.

2. **Attractor's light-age colouring (white leading edge, cool afterglow) → Magnetosphere, Current, Physarum**, because it makes *direction of motion* visible in every stroke. Current's single hue would gain a dimension, and Magnetosphere's tangle would read as flow instead of hair.

3. **Scanlines' rotating hidden image → Coral, Chladni, Flyover**, because swapping the underlying "picture" every minute or so (pond, dunes, bubbles) gives a flat simulation a slow narrative. Coral's feed/kill map could slowly become a spiral, then a face-free mandala, then a coastline.

4. **Abyss's three-depth parallax motes → Attractor, Current, Coral, Physarum, Chladni**, because a few drifting particles at three speeds is the cheapest possible cure for "floats in a void", and it gives the hats somewhere to sparkle.

5. **Koi's travelling ripple rings as the kick → Bloom, Abyss, Sumi**, because Koi's rings refract the world beneath them and fade outward, which is confined, legible and soft. Bloom's flat UI-like ellipses and Abyss's gold halo rings would both be improved by it.

6. **Mandala's feedback flight (each beat's image flies outward and ages) → Orphism, Chladni, Suprematism**, because Orphism's discs flying outward from the centre and aging from saturated to faded would turn the collection's best range palette into a tunnel. Suprematism's forms flying toward the viewer would make "weightless" literal.

7. **Iris's Droste pupil → Stargate's gate, Ophanim's core, Mandala's centre**, because a window into another scene at the vanishing point is the purest "somewhere to fall into". Stargate's gate opening onto Nebula, or onto a small Mandala, would be unforgettable.

8. **Chomper's world-folds-into-an-object drop → Iso City, Chladni, Koi**, because the moment the maze curls into a spinning torus is the collection's best surprise. Iso City wrapping into a tiny planet at the drop, or Chladni's plate curling into a sphere, would give the drop a *transformation* rather than just more of the same.

9. **Mobile's real soft shadows → Suprematism, Cubist, Stamps**, because a shadow falling on paper gives flat graphic forms depth and a light source, which is what the poster scenes most lack. Suprematism's black square casting a slow-swinging shadow is Malevich made kinetic.

10. **Stargate's "bass sets flight speed" → Attractor, Physarum, Impasto**, because a slow music-driven camera drift over the slime network, or a gentle push toward the attractor's centre, lets the music change *speed*, never jerk it, in scenes that currently don't travel at all.

## 7. New ideas

1. **Reef Tunnel.** *Crossing Coral with Mandala.* The look: Gray-Scott labyrinths growing on the inside walls of a feedback tunnel, salmon on teal, the maze stretching into long braids as it streams past. The music: kick seeds a blot at the vanishing point that grows labyrinth as it flies outward; bass sets the flight speed; the snare flips the feed/kill map from maze to spots in one ring of the tunnel only; the breakdown slows until the walls knit shut into a quiet maze.

2. **Strange Aurora.** *Crossing Attractor with Aurora.* The look: the attractor's silk ribbons hang as curtains over Aurora's mountain lake, with the lake doubling them and stars wheeling behind, in the attractor's champagne and blue, not green. The music: bass sweeps the coefficients so the curtain morphs; each kick lights a white leading edge that runs along one ribbon and reflects; the drop adds a second, warmer attractor behind the first.

3. **Droste Garden.** *Crossing Iris with Bloom.* The look: a slow zoom into a mandala flower whose pistil is a window onto another night garden, which contains another flower, forever, like a garden folded into itself. The music: each phrase completes one Droste step; the kick flares one petal ring; hats are pollen; the drop speeds the fall and opens more petal rings.

4. **Silk Corridor.** *Crossing Current with Stargate.* The look: Current's curl-noise streamlines painted onto the two slit-scan walls instead of Stargate's straight filaments, so the corridor is made of smoke, in a single deep hue that changes by section. The music: bass sets flight speed; the snare makes a whirlpool drain open on one wall that you fly past; the breakdown lets the strands lengthen into calm parallel silk.

5. **Sand Rose.** *Crossing Chladni with Zen and Mandala.* The look: a raked-sand garden seen from above, mirrored six ways, where the rake lines are Chladni nodal lines and the stones sit at the antinodes, in daylight matte sand and slate. The music: the snare changes mode and every grain flows to a new rose; the kick drops one pebble whose ripples rake outward; the breakdown gives a fine lattice.

6. **Delaunay Tunnel.** *Crossing Orphism with Mandala, minus the text.* The look: flat, matte, paper-grained discs of Sonia Delaunay colour flying outward from a vanishing point, each ring turning at its own speed, with no glow at all. It's range-compliant psychedelia. The music: kick stamps a new disc at the centre; bass sets rotation; the snare swaps one ring's colours with its neighbour's; the drop moves through three fresh palettes in the style of Oil.

7. **Engraved Deep.** *Crossing Thresholds with Abyss.* The look: jellyfish drawn in Thresholds' engraving (fine parallel lines, two inks, cream on black) sinking past through a column of engraved marine snow, with god rays rendered as hatching. The music: kick hatches one bell denser (darker, not whiter); the snare inverts one jelly's ink; the drop flips the whole ground from dark to cream like Thresholds does.

8. **Koi Sky.** *Crossing Koi with Low Poly.* The look: we lie at the bottom of the pond looking up, with koi passing overhead as silhouettes against a caustic-lit surface, and beyond the surface the low-poly sunset and a flying whale. The music: kicks are raindrops ringing the surface; bass swells the caustics; the drop brings a school of fry overhead; the breakdown clears the water to glass.

9. **Vein City.** *Crossing Physarum with Expressway and Night Train.* The look: flying low over a night landscape whose roads are a living slime network, with headlights as agents streaming along the veins, towns as food nodes glowing warm, and parallax haze. The music: the kick drops a new town that the network reaches for; the snare sends a pulse of light along the main roads; bass sets altitude and speed.

10. **Loom.** *Crossing Knit with Interference.* The look: two sheets of real knitted fabric, one indigo and one cream, drifting over each other so moiré fringes bloom across the stitches. It's op-art made soft and matte. The music: the kick tugs one row of stitches, which ripples a phase through the moiré; bass slowly rotates the top sheet; the drop adds a third colour of yarn.
