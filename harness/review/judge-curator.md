# Judge: The Curator

## 1. Who I am

I hang light and moving image the way Turrell, Agnes Martin, Eliasson and Ikeda taught a generation of curators to: one idea, fully realised, given room, with the material doing the work. I am the panel's editor. My job is to say what stays, what goes, and where a collection of 72 turns out to be about 25 scenes plus a lot of variations.

A note on bias: TASTE.md asks for "rich, layered", and I am here to push back on the second half of that. The strongest scenes in this set are not the most layered. They are the ones that know what to leave out.

## 3. My top 10

**1. Letterpress (10).** Every other scene adds for the drop; Letterpress is the only one that subtracts afterwards. It opens on a nearly empty sheet of cotton stock, and the type arrives as blind impression: shadow, not ink, a relief you see only because a lamp rakes across it. The drop is the platen finally inking the forme, two colours and a giant red ampersand. Then, at the breakdown, the sheet is blank again, and SWAY rises out of the paper uninked. That return to nothing is the bravest thing in the collection. It trusts silence to be part of the music. This is the scene I would show a sceptic.

**2. Mobile (9).** A Calder in a white gallery. There is no glow and no particles; the second layer is simply the shadow the object throws on the wall and the floor. The drop unfolds more arms and the camera draws back. The breakdown lets it recede until only a handful of leaves hang at the top of an almost white frame (24 s). It is the only scene that uses the white room as a material, not a background, and it proves the collection doesn't need darkness to hold a room.

**3. Attractor (9).** One orbit, photographed as a long exposure, coloured by the age of its light: warm leading edges and a cool afterglow. It floats on black with no backdrop, and it doesn't need one. The breakdown thins the ribbons to single threads you could count. It is the proof that a single mathematical object, lit well, beats a stack of layers.

**4. Interference (9).** Two sheets of black and white lines and a trace of red, and the music makes the moiré. The kick is literally a phase shift of one sheet, which the eye magnifies into a bloom of fringes, so the reaction is optical rather than decorative. It is the scene that taught the collection legibility, and it did it with two materials and one accent.

**5. Sumi (9).** A handscroll unrolling: grey washes, one red sun, a small boat. The kick is confined to rings spreading from one hull; the snare is a single drop of ink marbling on the river. The composition leaves two thirds of the frame to paper and mist. My one objection is the red lantern glow in the drop, a soft additive blob in a scene where nothing else glows. Replace it with a flat red dot that grows, and this becomes a 10.

**6. Poster (8).** A Swiss concert poster whose sections are words: KLANG, LICHT, NACHT. The kick snaps one token to a new cell, the way limited animation moves one cel. The breakdown frame (NACHT, a red disc, a black square, three arcs) could hang in the Museum für Gestaltung. The drop's ticker band and field of tokens are the only clutter; cut them to one line.

**7. Chladni (8).** Sand on a plate finds a figure, loses it when struck, and finds a new one. The kick bursts sand at one spot, and the whole plate churns only when the mode changes. Its real achievement is the breakdown: the sand settling into a new diagonal lattice, which is the best section change in the set. The churn in the drop could be quieter. Restraint here means letting the figure be legible more of the time.

**8. Zen (8).** A karesansui from above in afternoon light, with a rake that is always working, a figure with a job rather than a reaction. The kick presses one ring around one stone. It is matte, shadowed and daylit, with nothing additive in it. My only note is housekeeping: the maple leaves pile up and are never swept, so minute five will be busier than minute one. The rake should rake them.

**9. Koi (8).** A shallow pond in sun: caustics, lily pads, fish with real undulation, and a kick that arrives as rain on one wandering spot and travels outward. The drop brings a school of fry in one quadrant, not a flood. It is the collection's best demonstration that "light" can mean daylight through water and not bloom.

**10. Knit (7).** It barely moves, and that is its virtue. A Fair Isle panel grows row by row, a window's light sweeps across it in the drop, and the drop leaves a permanent red band in the fabric. The music becomes an object with a history. It is the only scene where you can read the past eight minutes of the set in the image, and that idea should travel (see Transplants).

Honourable mention: **Suprematism**. Its intro and its ghost-square breakdown are both top-ten material; the confetti drop keeps it out.

## 4. Cut or merge

Be decisive: a VJ set with 72 options has none. The cuts below take the collection to about 40, and the core in section 5 takes it to 20.

**Cut outright**
1. **Text**: a logo. If a title card is wanted, Letterpress is a better one.
2. **Dot Matrix**: a sign. Same reason.
3. **Twinkle Toph**: a private portrait on a white field. Keep it for the family, off the stage.
4. **Parametric Lines**: invisible most of the time. Nothing to rescue.
5. **Descent**: muddy, blown out, illegible, and the collection already has seven other tunnels.
6. **Iris**: a staring frontal eye is the wrong thing to put in front of an altered room, and TASTE.md itself warns against menace.
7. **Grunge**: cacophony. Zine does the genre with one quiet moment; Grunge has none.
8. **Republic**: an info-dump. Irony doesn't excuse clutter.
9. **Iso City**: no negative space at all. Cube and Isle do the toy-object idea better.
10. **Diner, Low Poly**: kitsch that doesn't change between sections.
11. **Clay, Bloom**: cute without an idea. Bloom's flowers are clip-art and its rings are UI.

**Merge**
12. **Rings + Arcs + Jags → one scene, "Line"**: the three early sketches share the collection's most minimalist instinct, and each is too small to stand. One scene: a single hand-drawn line (arc, ring or zigzag, chosen by a control) at full stage scale on parchment or black. See New ideas 2.
13. **Flyover → Scanlines**: the same terrain-line idea. Scanlines keeps the travelling kick ring; offer Flyover's plain white-on-black as a Scanlines palette.
14. **Magnetosphere → Current / Attractor**: a third flow field in the same register. Its two-charge colour could be an Attractor palette option.
15. **Op Art → Interference**: Interference is the op-art scene. Op Art adds a headline and eye strain.
16. **Mandala + Ophanim**: three centred radial icons (with Iris cut) is two too many. Keep Mandala as the collection's single honest trance tunnel, and fold Ophanim's wheels of eyes in as a Mandala "icon" variant.
17. **Nebula + Stargate + Expressway**: three glowing flights down a tube. Keep Stargate as the quotation. Expressway's great idea (entering a tunnel *as* the drop) should move to a scene with a better tunnel. Cut Nebula.
18. **Vorticism + Futurist + Mass Poster + Constructivist → Constructivist**: four loud red-and-black manifestos. Constructivist has the best exhale (the dancers leave). Keep one.
19. **Pop + Zine + Penny Dreadful → Zine**: three dense printed pages. Zine's 3 AM breakdown is the only rest among them.
20. **WPA + Magazine Cover**: two handsome illustrations that barely move. Cut both. Their dusk palettes can live in Aurora.
21. **Isle → Cube**: two toy-objects-on-plinths. Cube's instrument-panel idea is stronger.
22. **Rave**: an illustration of the thing that is already happening in the room. Cut.

## 6. Themes

**1. The drop is where the collection loses its nerve.** Look along the middle row of almost any sheet: Murmuration paints its flock rainbow, Terminal swaps amber for a spectrum canyon, Skyline fills the whole sky, Coral fills the whole frame, Suprematism throws confetti, Plate adds triangles, Physarum adds a second species. The intros and breakdowns are consistently better than the drops. TASTE.md says "the drop is obviously bigger", and the scenes have read "bigger" as "more". Letterpress and De Stijl show the alternative: the drop as a *change of state* (ink arrives; the grid tilts), not an increase in quantity.

**2. The poster has eaten the collection.** About 27 of the 72 scenes are posters, and in most of them type takes 30–60% of the frame. Type is read, not watched, and a dark room at 2 am does not want to read. The posters that work (Poster, Letterpress, Suprematism) treat the word as a shape and change it at section boundaries only. The ones that fail (Penny Dreadful, Republic, Grunge, Cubist, Orphism's column) set body copy nobody will ever read.

**3. Eight ways to fly down a tube.** Stargate, Descent, Nebula, Expressway, Terminal, Thresholds, Mandala, and Iris's pupil are all tunnels. The first four are glowing tunnels. The altered viewer does love a tunnel, but a collection needs one great one, not eight adequate ones. Thresholds is the only tunnel with a material of its own (woodcut) and a reason (doors).

**4. Centred icons.** Mandala, Iris, Ophanim, Orphism and Descent all put a radial object in the dead centre and pulse it. That is exactly the "centred shape pulsing to the kick" TASTE.md warns against, dressed in gold leaf. Mobile, Sumi and Poster all compose off-centre, and they are calmer and more alive for it.

**5. Glow survives where it was banned.** TASTE.md retired glow as the default finish, but it keeps leaking into matte scenes as a single blob: Sumi's lantern, Aurora's white-hot kick spot, Abyss's halo rings, Paper's backlit flats (Paper earns it; the others don't). In a matte scene one additive blob reads as a rendering error, not as light.

**6. Accumulation without erasure.** Zen's leaves, Stamps' prints, Chomper's flowers, Coral's maze and Knit's rows all remember the music. That is the collection's most original idea: an image that is a record of the set so far. But only Letterpress and Suprematism ever *erase*. Without an erasure the memory saturates, and minute five is busier than minute one, which is exactly what TASTE.md says to avoid.

**7. Palette-per-section as hue cycling by other means.** Nebula (red, violet, teal), Rave (teal, magenta, red), Scanlines (green, magenta, blue), Oil (CMY to RGB) and Stargate (blue, gold, blue) mark their sections by swapping the whole palette. That is legible, but it is `hue = section` instead of `hue = frameCount`. The better device is a single accent that appears (Interference's red, Letterpress's red ink, Sumi's red sun).

**8. The sketches knew something.** Arcs, Jags and Rings, the oldest scenes, are the only truly minimal work here: one line, one colour, a lot of space. They failed on scale and legibility, not on idea, and the collection walked away from that register instead of fixing them.

## 7. Transplants

1. **The return to blank from Letterpress → Stamps, Zen, Coral, Chomper**, because each of these accumulates and never clears. Give each breakdown an erasure: a fresh sheet slides in under the dice, the rake finally sweeps the leaves and redraws the grooves, the reef bleaches back to sparse spots, the maze is re-laid with pellets. Memory with forgetting is a cycle; memory without it is saturation.

2. **The after-image from Suprematism → Poster, De Stijl, Constructivist**, because the ghost-white square left where the black square was is the most poetic breakdown device in the set. Let each outgoing headline or bar leave a pale blind shape on the paper for a few bars. The page then remembers its previous section.

3. **The confined kick from Sumi → Aurora and Abyss**, because rings spreading from one small, specific thing (the hull) are fully legible without touching the rest of the frame. Replace Aurora's white blotch with a single fold of curtain brightening and its reflection answering on the lake. Replace Abyss's halo rings with one bell contracting.

4. **Cast shadow from Mobile as the second layer → Suprematism, Poster, Cube, Liquid Glass**, because a real soft shadow on the ground is depth without glow. Let Suprematism's forms lift off the sheet and throw shadows that lengthen with the bass. It is exactly the "weightless" its headline promises.

5. **The fabric that records the drop, from Knit → Physarum and Impasto**, because a lasting trace of the drop turns a loop into a history. Physarum's network could keep a thicker, brighter trunk wherever the drop's food landed. Impasto's wheat could keep the flattened, pale swathes the drop's gusts cut.

6. **Structural change instead of additive change, from De Stijl → Poster, Interference, Chladni**, because tilting the whole panel 45° on the drop is bigger than any number of added tokens, and it adds nothing. Poster's grid could rotate; Interference's sheets could swing from diagonal to vertical; Chladni's plate could turn a quarter.

7. **The single accent colour of Interference → Murmuration, Physarum, Terminal**, because one red thread in black and white is louder than a spectrum. Murmuration's drop keeps the black flock and lets only the sun's rim flare red. Physarum stays teal, with amber food nodes as its only accent. Terminal stays amber, with one red character.

8. **Colour from the age of the light, from Attractor → Current and Magnetosphere**, because colour derived from time (white when new, cool in afterglow) is chosen colour that no designer has to pick. Current's single hue would gain a white leading edge and so read as flow rather than wire.

9. **The section change as a new stable state, from Chladni → Scanlines and Coral**, because sand settling into a new figure is a better breakdown than a palette swap. Scanlines should change its hidden image at each section and keep one palette. Coral should change its reaction parameters and let the pattern re-form.

10. **The drop as a place you enter, from Expressway → Stargate, Thresholds, Paper**, because driving *into* a tunnel on the drop and out of it at the breakdown is the best dramaturgy in the set. Stargate could begin in open night sky and only enter the corridor at the drop.

## 8. New ideas

**1. Skyspace.** *Lineage: Turrell's Skyspaces; Mobile's white room; Sumi's single red sun.* A flat, smooth ceiling of one colour with a sharp square aperture cut in it, through which we see sky. The ceiling's colour is fixed and the sky's colour drifts, so simultaneous contrast does the work: the sky seems to change as the ceiling pushes against it. *Music:* the pad sets the sky's hue and value; the kick sends one bird, or one star, across the aperture (confined, and never the same spot twice); the drop brings the sky to near-equal luminance with the ceiling, so the edge of the aperture seems to dissolve (the Turrell effect); the breakdown restores the edge. There is almost nothing in the frame, and a high viewer will watch it for ten minutes.

**2. Line.** *Lineage: Arcs, Jags and Rings, rescued; Agnes Martin; Letterpress's paper.* A pale canvas ground and hand-ruled graphite lines, drawn one at a time across the full width, each slightly wavering like a real pencil against a ruler. *Music:* the hats draw the lines, so tempo becomes density; the kick lays a thin wash of colour (pale rose, pale blue) into one band between two lines; the drop allows one band to deepen to a saturated stripe; the breakdown erases from the top down with a soft eraser, leaving the ghost grooves. It is the Letterpress arc in pencil.

**3. Datum.** *Lineage: Ryoji Ikeda's test pattern and datamatics; Interference's black and white; Glitch's confined damage.* A single white horizontal hairline on black, dead centre, with tiny numerical ticks beneath it. *Music:* the kick makes one vertical cut through the line at a new position, which stays for a bar; the hats add sub-pixel flicker to the ticks; the drop splits the line into a barcode field that scrolls at tempo, full width, in pure black and white; the breakdown collapses the field back to one line. No colour at all, or one red cut per phrase.

**4. Weather.** *Lineage: Eliasson's Weather Project; Sumi's red sun; Suprematism's disc.* A huge low sun disc, monochrome yellow-orange, in a hall of haze; a mirrored ceiling doubles it into a full circle; tiny silhouettes lie on the floor far below. *Music:* the bass sets the haze density, so the disc blooms and sharpens as the low end rises and falls, the only "glow" and a physical one; the kick raises one silhouette's arm (one figure, never the crowd); the drop thins the haze until the sun is almost a hard disc; the breakdown thickens it until it is nearly gone.

**5. Ripple Tank.** *Lineage: Koi's rain; Interference's fringes; Chladni's physics.* Still black water seen from directly above, lit by a single raking light so only the ripples show, as fine silver lines. *Music:* each kick drops one drop at a new point, and the rings from successive drops interfere, making real interference fringes; the snare drops two at once, so two-source interference makes the classic hyperbolic nodal lines; the bass sets the wave speed; the drop drops at the beat subdivision, so the whole surface becomes a lattice; the breakdown lets the water go flat until only one ring crosses it.

**6. Seascape.** *Lineage: Hiroshi Sugimoto's seascapes; Murmuration's horizon; Sumi's greys.* A horizon exactly at half height, grey sea below and grey sky above, the only thing in the frame, filmed as a slow tracking shot so the swell moves past. *Music:* the bass sets the swell height; the kick sends one crest line of white foam along a single wave, left to right; the drop lifts the light in the sky so the horizon line sharpens and the sea darkens (contrast is the only escalation); the breakdown brings in mist until the horizon disappears altogether for a bar. A scene you put on at 5 am.

**7. Harmonograph.** *Lineage: Attractor's single orbit; Planets' pendulums; Letterpress's paper and lamp.* A pen on a pendulum drawing a harmonograph figure on cream paper, in one ink, the drawing slowly decaying inward as the pendulums lose energy. *Music:* each kick nudges one pendulum, so the figure bends from there on; the bass sets the damping, so loud passages sustain the figure and quiet ones let it spiral in; the drop adds a second pen in red; the breakdown lifts the pens and slides a fresh sheet in, keeping the last drawing faintly visible beneath it.

**8. Fold.** *Lineage: Letterpress's blind relief; Mobile's shadow; Paper's cut sheets.* A single sheet of heavy white paper under one low lamp, folding itself, one crease at a time, into an origami form (a crane, a star, a tessellation), then unfolding. The creases stay as ridges in the unfolded paper. *Music:* each kick makes one fold, with a crisp little settle; the pad swings the lamp slowly so the shadows move; the drop completes the form and rotates it once; the breakdown unfolds it flat, leaving a sheet covered in the ridges of everything it has been. A white-on-white scene where all the drama is shadow.
