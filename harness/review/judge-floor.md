# Judge: The Floor

## 1. Who I am

I've run the lighting desk in dark rooms for twenty years. I judge a visual by one test: does it still work at 2 a.m. from the back of the room on a washed-out projector, with the DJ three tunes into a peak? I don't care what it's quoting. I care whether the kick reads without giving everyone a headache, whether the drop gets a reaction from the floor, whether it's too bright to keep the room dark, and whether I'd still reach for it an hour in.

A note on my bias before the numbers. TASTE.md's "range, not a house style" push is right as art direction, but it has a cost the doc doesn't price in: **cream paper is a floodlight.** Half of batches 4 and 5 are daylight or parchment grounds. On a projector in a dark room, that throws a beige wash over the dancefloor and kills the club lighting. I've marked those down for the room, not for the design. Many of them would be 8s in a gallery or a daytime garden party.

## 3. My top 10

**1. Expressway (9).** This is how a set should feel. The build is a wet night drive with real reflections. At 10 s you enter a neon tunnel of rectangles and radial laser lines, and at the breakdown you drive out back into the city. The 96 s sheet shows the pattern holding: tunnel on every drop, city on every breakdown. The floor learns that language within two drops and starts anticipating it, which is the best thing a visual can do. It's dark enough to keep the room dark, and the motion is steady forward travel, never a jolt.

**2. Chomper (9).** I'd have skipped past a Pac-Man maze in the menu, and I'd have been wrong. At the drop the flat maze peels up into a spinning torus (10 to 16 s). Later drops become a 3D fly-through (36 s) and a tunnel ride (60 s), and by 84 to 96 s it has swapped to terracotta. The breakdown fills the maze with flowers. It's playful, it reads from across the room because the teal walls are thick, and it keeps surprising you over 96 s. My only complaint is the cream corridors, but they're broken up enough by the teal that it isn't a floodlight.

**3. Rave (9).** Showing a crowd at a rave to a crowd at a rave shouldn't work, and it absolutely does. Lasers fan out over silhouetted hands. Each drop brings a new palette (green at 12 s, red and amber at 36 s, pink and violet at 84 s) and the breakdown drops to a hazy amber. Everything stays in the top half of the frame, and the crowd along the bottom anchors it. This is my peak-hour default.

**4. Thresholds (8).** It's black and white engraving with no glow, and it's the best tunnel in the collection. Doors, gears, film reels and wax seals rush past, and the 96 s sheet never shows the same room twice. It's hypnotic without being tiring. It proves TASTE.md's range argument better than any cream poster does. Dock it one point because the drop's light cream fill brightens the room a lot.

**5. Skyline (8).** This is the textbook for "kick visible but confined." Each kick is one shell bursting in one place, the drop is the full barrage, and the breakdown is lone shells over the reflection. Anybody gets it instantly. Fix the 84 s drop, where the stacked blooms wash pink-white across the sky.

**6. Scanlines (8).** The kick is a glowing ring that rolls across the terrain (12 and 60 s). That's the ideal: you see every beat and it moves only part of the frame. Each section has its own palette: green intro, pink-grid drop, blue sine hills in the breakdown, orange terrain later. It's dark and reads as landscape from any distance. It could stay up for twenty minutes.

**7. Ophanim (8).** A symbol that reads from the smoking area: wings, rotating gold rings of eyes and a starburst heart on navy. Symmetry is catnip for the altered. On a projector it's the strongest single image in the set. It sits at 8 and not 9 because the 96 s sheet shows the same picture throughout. It needs somewhere to go on the third drop.

**8. Abyss (8).** The prettiest dark-room scene: jellyfish in shafts of light, drifting across the frame, with halo rings on the kick. The intro frames at 48, 72 and 96 s are sublime. Close-up bells clip to white in the drops, so clamp the highlights and it's a 9.

**9. Terminal (7, but a top-10 idea).** The amber ASCII sun is perfect for the room when nothing's happening. On the drop it turns into a colour ASCII flight down a road with a torus overhead (12, 36, 60 and 84 s), and at the breakdown it drops back to amber. One set of rules, two very different moods, and dark throughout. This is the shape I want more scenes to copy.

**10. Constructivist (7).** It's cream, and I still love it. The ticker DANCE UNTIL THE MORNING rolls along the diagonal, and on the drop three stick dancers walk on and dance. They leave at the breakdown, with one lone dancer still going at 20 s. People will point at the screen and laugh. That's a reaction too.

## 4. Weakest 8

**Arcs (2).** Correction: my first pass called it broken because every even-second frame was black. It isn't broken. Each arc sweeps over exactly 1 s, so frames on whole seconds catch every arc at zero length. The offset render (`harness/renders/orig/arcs-offset/sheet.png`, frames at x.5 s) shows what's actually there: one small white crescent in dead centre, about 1% of the frame. It's slightly thicker in the drop (10.5 s, 12.5 s), and that's the only difference between sections. The sweep resetting to nothing once a second is still a real problem: at the back of the room it reads as a flicker, or as the screen dropping out. **Fix:** stop the arcs collapsing to zero. Let old arcs linger and fade while the new one draws, so there's always a trail. Scale it up so the arcs span most of the frame height, and let the drop add more concentric arcs in a second colour rather than just thickening the one.

**Rings (2).** The whole visual occupies a coin in the centre. **Fix:** scale it until the outer ring sits at the frame edge. Let the kick send a ring out from the centre to the edges and fade (Scanlines' travelling ring, but top-down). Put a fog or haze plane behind it so there's something in the other 95% of the frame.

**Parametric Lines (2).** Beautiful string-art for one frame (10 s), faint to invisible otherwise. **Fix:** make the lines thicker and brighter at the base level. Keep a stable figure that morphs slowly instead of redrawing, and use a limited two-ink palette so the drop can add the third colour.

**Twinkle Toph (2).** A white page with one person's portrait on it. **Fix:** invert it: pale dots on black, with a warm ink. Better still, make the stipple portrait a *template* that takes any image. As a birthday-party one-off it's fine; as a set scene, it isn't.

**Jags (2).** Zigzag lines across a cream field. **Fix:** go dark. Stack the jags into several parallax layers like a mountain range or seismograph, and let the kick send a spike travelling along one line rather than thickening all of them.

**Op Art (3).** A full-frame high-contrast black and white checker that warps and pulses is exactly the large-area flicker the "altered viewer" section warns about, and on a projector it aliases into shimmer. The words (SEE THE SOUND, RETINA DANCE) are illegible because of the effect itself. **Fix:** coarsen the checker at least 3×, bring the contrast down to warm grey on off-black, and confine the warp to the one bulge. Let the kick move the bulge rather than re-vibrating the whole field. Drop the words.

**Cubist (3).** Beige on beige, with changes in the collage you'd need a loupe to find. The 10 to 16 s drop only adds a few muted facets. **Fix:** in the drop, let the facets actually break and slide apart, so the still life shatters into planes and reassembles on the breakdown, and bring in one saturated colour (the red of DANSE, used in the planes too). Put a dark ground behind the oval.

**Text (2).** A logo. **Fix:** fold it into Dot Matrix as a mode, or turn it into a "message" scene that takes a line the DJ types and sets it big (Direct Address already shows type on black reads from the bar).

(Honourable mention: **Planets** at 3. Double the scale and add a warm sun as a second light, and it's a 6.)

## 5. Themes

1. **The scenes that win change *place* at the drop, not brightness.** Expressway enters a tunnel, Chomper folds into a torus, Terminal switches from sun to flight, De Stijl rotates 45°, Letterpress inks. The floor reads a new world as "the drop" far better than it reads "everything got 30% brighter." Weaker scenes (Coral, Knit, Plate, Diner) just add more of the same.

2. **Cream is the new rainbow.** The last batch overcorrected. Constructivist, Suprematism, Cubist, Poster, Liquid Glass, Glitch, Letterpress, Stamps, Plate, Mobile, Isle, WPA, Sumi and Zen are all bright neutral grounds. In a dark room that's the single biggest real-world problem in the collection. Range doesn't have to mean daylight: Thresholds, Terminal, Dot Matrix and Scanlines prove you can do limited ink, print and texture on a dark ground.

3. **Small type is dead type.** Set-list cards, captions, bylines and "une soirée de musique" all vanish past three metres. Only type set at 20% or more of the frame height works (Direct Address, Futurist's BOMM, Letterpress's DANCE, Constructivist's ticker). Everything smaller is decoration at best.

4. **The confined kick is solved, but only in a few places.** Skyline's single shell, Scanlines' rolling ring, Zen's rings in the sand, Koi's ripples, Aurora's ripples on the lake and Futurist's exploding word are all excellent. Many scenes still have no identifiable kick at all (Current, Magnetosphere, Coral, Physarum), or the kick is too small to see from the back (Sumi's boat ripple, Cube).

5. **Drops clip.** Abyss, Skyline (84 s), Murmuration (36, 60 and 84 s) and Descent all blow out to white at peak. On a projector clipped white is the brightest thing in the building, and it flattens the image just when you want the most detail.

6. **Longevity depends on per-drop variation.** Rave (new palette each drop), Chomper (new geometry each drop), Scanlines (rotating palettes) and Thresholds (new rooms) all survive 96 s. Aurora, Ophanim, Expressway and Skyline do the same thing every drop; that's fine for 10 minutes and a problem at 40.

7. **The retro-figurative scenes are the crowd-pleasers.** Rave, Pop, Mass Poster, Constructivist, Clay and Night Train get a *laugh* or a cheer, and the abstract ones never will. A two-hour set needs both.

8. **The originals (Text through Dot Matrix) are the family's old guard, and it shows.** Most of them are one effect, small, and far below the bar the later batches set. They should be retired, rescued into presets of better scenes, or rebuilt.

## 6. Transplants

1. **Per-drop palette rotation from Rave → Aurora, Ophanim, Skyline, Stargate.** Rave's drops go green, then red-amber, then pink. Aurora's green wall and Ophanim's fixed gold need a second and third outfit so the 40th minute doesn't look like the 4th.

2. **The travelling kick ring from Scanlines → Rings, Chladni, Koi, Physarum.** A kick that sets off a ring rolling through the world is legible and confined. In Physarum the network could pulse outward along its tendrils from the ring. In Chladni the sand could jump as the ring passes.

3. **The place change from Expressway (tunnel on the drop) → Nebula, Descent, Night Train, Flyover.** Night Train should go into a tunnel or onto a bridge at the drop and out again at the breakdown; it already hints at this with a bridge. Flyover should drop into a canyon.

4. **The two-state sun and flight from Terminal → Dot Matrix, Thresholds.** Dot Matrix should become the ticker at rest and a colour LED-wall flight on the drop. Thresholds could flip to a warm sepia ink on the drop instead of brightening the ground.

5. **Onomatopoeia on the kick from Futurist and Pop → Constructivist, Vorticism, Grunge, Zine.** A word bursting out of one spot on the beat is the most legible kick we have that isn't light. Grunge's FEEDBACK, STATIC and HUM should *be* hits, stamped on the kick, rather than slow crossfades.

6. **Crowd arrival from Constructivist's dancers and Clay's blobs → Zagreb, Magazine Cover, Isle, Night Train.** Things walking on for the drop and leaving at the breakdown is a readable storyline. The Magazine Cover dancers in the drop are too small: bring them to the foreground at double scale.

7. **Highlight roll-off from Current and Thresholds (they never clip) → Abyss, Skyline, Murmuration, Descent.** Tone-map the peak so the brightest bell or firework is 90% white, never flat 100%, and keep detail in the drop.

8. **The dark ground from Diner and Terminal → the cream poster family (Suprematism, Poster, Constructivist, Liquid Glass).** Add a "night print" mode to each: the same composition inked on near-black paper, with red and cream as the inks. Keep daylight as an option for daytime sets.

9. **Section-as-word from Poster (KLANG, LICHT, NACHT) → Direct Address, Letterpress, Glitch.** Letting the words change with the song section is clever and cheap. Letterpress already does it; Glitch should stop saying SIGNAL forever.

10. **Water that reacts to the kick, from Koi, Aurora and Sumi → Skyline, Diner.** Skyline's reflection and Diner's wet asphalt should ripple on the kick. Diner badly needs a kick; a puddle ripple through the neon reflection is the obvious one.

## 7. New ideas

1. **Night Market (Crossing Expressway with Diner and Night Train).** A slow tram ride down a night-market street of neon signs, food-stall steam and paper lanterns. The kick makes one lantern swing in the foreground, hats set the signs flickering, and on the drop the tram enters a covered arcade of neon (Expressway's tunnel, in warm tungsten instead of rainbow) and leaves at the breakdown.

2. **Engraved Deep (Crossing Thresholds with Abyss).** A descent through an ocean drawn as a Victorian engraving: hatched water, a diving bell, jellyfish in line-work, all in sepia and cream on black. The kick is one jellyfish contracting. On the drop a whale's hatched flank slides past close enough to fill the frame. The breakdown is empty water and falling marine snow.

3. **Pinball (Crossing Chomper with Pop).** A top-down pinball table, flat and graphic. The kick fires a flipper, snares light bumpers with BZZT and BLAM, and hats set the rollover lights chasing. On the drop the camera tilts into a 3D ride down the ramp, like Chomper's fold, and at the breakdown the ball rests in the lane.

4. **Laser Congregation (Crossing Rave with Ophanim).** A cathedral nave in silhouette, with Ophanim's ring of eyes as the rose window. Lasers sweep through the incense haze from the window, and the crowd's hands are in the pews. The kick is one laser fan. On the drop the rose window opens and turns, and the breakdown is candlelight in haze.

5. **Sand Dancers (Crossing Zen with Constructivist's dancers).** A top-down raked garden where footprints appear and trace dance steps on each kick. Two, three, then eight invisible dancers during the drop leave overlapping circles and rakes. The breakdown slowly rakes it smooth. It's the night drawn as a record in the sand.

6. **Chladni Stage (Crossing Chladni with Rave).** A vibrating plate seen from low, as a stage floor, with sand leaping in columns lit from the side by coloured spotlights. Each section settles into a new nodal pattern. The kick throws a burst of sand up in one antinode, and the drop brings the spots in. It's dark ground, gold sand and light beams.

7. **Fireworks Letterpress (Crossing Letterpress with Skyline).** A dark press sheet where each kick "prints" a firework as a coloured stamp of ink: one stamp, one kick, in two or three inks. On the drop the sheet fills in overlapping registration, and at the breakdown it's pulled off the press to a fresh black sheet. That fixes both Letterpress's blank breakdown and its cream ground.

8. **ASCII Aurora (Crossing Terminal with Aurora).** The aurora and mirror lake drawn in glyphs on black. Hats sparkle as character flicker, the kick sends a ripple of characters across the lake, and on the drop the glyphs switch from green phosphor to full colour, the way Terminal's flight does.

9. **Flipbook (Crossing Clay and Constructivist with Pop).** A kid's flipbook in the corner of a notebook: a stick figure dancing, with one page flipped per beat. At the build the pages flip faster; on the drop the figure leaps off the page into a full-frame ink crowd; at the breakdown it's back to one figure, walking home.
