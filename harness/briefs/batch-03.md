# Batch 03

Ten new directions, chosen to avoid every technique and aesthetic in batches
01–02 and the 2016 originals. Many of them travel, because Raph asked for more
sense of movement. The beat stays visible but confined (see `TASTE.md`:
"Kick → visible but confined", checked with `harness/jolt.mjs`).

## 01 · Night Train (`nighttrain`): pixel art and a CRT
A train ride at night through a sleeping world in 16-bit pixel art: parallax
layers of countryside, a town, a bridge, a station, moon and stars, drawn at a
low internal resolution with a real palette and dithering, then shown through
a gentle CRT (scanlines, slight bloom and curvature). **Movement:** the world
scrolls past the window at train speed, and the music sets the speed.
**Music:** kicks blink a single lit window or a signal lamp passing by; the
snare throws sparks from the rails; hats twinkle stars; the drop brings a
town's worth of lights and fireworks on the horizon.

## 02 · Sumi (`sumi`): ink wash
Japanese ink painting: mountains in receding washes of grey, mist, a river,
pine silhouettes, a small boat, on warm paper, with red seal accents allowed.
Brush marks with real wet edges and bleeding. **Movement:** a slow drift along
the river as mountains pass (a handscroll unrolling). **Music:** the snare drops
an ink bloom into the water; kicks ripple the boat's wake; the bass thickens
the mist; the drop brings a flight of birds or a flurry of calligraphic strokes.
Monochrome with one accent colour.

## 03 · Stargate (`stargate`): slit-scan corridor
The 2001 Stargate: an endless corridor of streaming light planes rushing past
on both sides (or above and below), made with the slit-scan idea (textures
smeared along the direction of travel), with shifting colour fields and
occasional structures. **Movement:** constant high-speed flight. **Music:** the
bass sets the speed; kicks flare one panel of the corridor; the snare sends a
new pattern down the walls; the drop shifts the colour fields and opens the
corridor into a vast space.

## 04 · Scanlines (`scanlines`): analog video synthesiser
The Rutt/Etra scan processor and Sandin Image Processor: a raster of horizontal
lines displaced vertically by the brightness of a hidden image (a face, a
landscape, a noise field, a shape), glowing like phosphor on a vector monitor,
with slight chroma separation and persistence. **Movement:** the hidden image
turns in 3D and the raster sweeps. **Music:** each band displaces its own group
of lines; kicks ripple one wave across the raster; the snare switches the
hidden image; the drop adds colour channels and doubles the raster.

## 05 · Chladni (`chladni`): cymatic sand
A vibrating plate covered in sand. Grains migrate toward the nodal lines of the
current vibration mode, so figures form, dissolve and re-form as the mode
changes. Use real Chladni mode formulas and thousands of grains, with a plate
material you choose (brass, black glass) and raking light. **Movement:** grains
flowing between figures. **Music:** the dominant frequency chooses the mode,
so the music literally draws the figure; kicks make grains hop in one region;
the snare changes the mode; the drop combines modes into complex figures.

## 06 · Physarum (`physarum`): slime mould
The agent-based slime-mould model (Jones 2010): hundreds of thousands of agents
depositing and following a trail map on the GPU, forming living vein
networks. Render it like bioluminescent mycelium or a glowing river delta at
night. **Movement:** the network grows, reroutes and pulses continuously.
**Music:** the bass changes the agents' sensor angle (the network's character);
kicks drop a food source that the network reaches toward; the snare sends a
pulse of light along the veins; the drop splits the population into two
coloured species.

## 07 · Expressway (`expressway`): a night drive
Driving through a neon city at night: an elevated expressway, tunnel lights,
signs and towers streaming past, tail-light and head-light streaks, bokeh,
rain on the windscreen with drops that refract the lights. **Movement:**
forward travel, with gentle curves and tunnels. **Music:** the bass sets the
speed; kicks pulse one row of tunnel lights; the snare sends a wiper sweep or
a passing truck's light wash across part of the view; hats glint in the rain;
the drop enters a tunnel of colour.

## 08 · Nebula (`nebula`): flight through gas clouds
Volumetric flight through a nebula: raymarched emission clouds lit from
within by young stars, dust lanes, and star clusters, with depth of field.
**Movement:** slow flight forward and banking through the clouds. **Music:** the
bass brightens the emission; kicks ignite a single star that lights its
surrounding cloud; the snare sends a shock front rippling through one region;
the drop accelerates the flight and shifts the gas from hydrogen red to
oxygen teal.

## 09 · Paper (`paper`): cut-paper theatre
A layered cut-paper diorama, in the tradition of Lotte Reiniger's silhouettes
and kirigami: forests, hills, a moon, creatures, each layer a sheet of paper
with soft drop shadows, visible paper grain and a gentle stop-motion wobble
(animated on twos). **Movement:** the camera travels through the layers, into
the forest. **Music:** kicks make one paper creature hop; the snare flips a
paper element (a flower opens, a bird takes off); the bass sways the trees; the
drop lights the scene from behind so the layers glow.

## 10 · Terminal (`terminal`): ASCII
Text-mode art: a 3D scene (a rotating torus, a landscape flyover, a tunnel, a
city) rendered in characters, shaded by a brightness ramp of glyphs, on a
phosphor terminal (amber or green) with glow, scanlines and a little
persistence. **Movement:** the 3D scene flies or turns. **Music:** kicks brighten
one region of glyphs; the snare glitches a few rows into other character sets;
hats flicker single characters; the drop switches the scene and adds colour.

## 11 · Thresholds (`thresholds`): the tunnel of opening doors
Raph's own sketch, one he has always wanted to see (2026-09-28): "flying
forward through a tunnel of successively opening doors. Gears, machinery,
steam. Many different kinds of doors mechanically opening before you. In my
mind I see it as a black and white drawing, or maybe an 'animated woodcut'."

**Look:** black ink on white (or white on black), rendered as a woodcut or
engraving: tone made entirely of carved lines whose width and density follow
the form (hatching that wraps around cylinders, radiates from hubs, follows
the curve of a gear tooth), bold black masses, gouge marks, slight paper
texture. No grey fills, no gradients: every tone is line. Crisp at projector
resolution. Optionally, very subtle animation "boil" in the line work, as in
hand-drawn animation, but never jitter.

**The doors:** a long corridor of successive doors, each a different mechanism,
each opening just in time for us to pass through: iris diaphragms, bank-vault
doors with radial bolts retracting, portcullises rising, double doors sliding
apart, bascule leaves lifting, shutters of slats rotating, spiral or helical
doors unscrewing, clamshells, rotating gear-toothed discs, doors that split into
many segments. Between them: gears meshing on the walls, pistons, chains,
cams, pipes, pressure gauges, and steam venting. Doors should feel heavy and
mechanical: they unlock, pause, and move with weight (ease-in, overshoot and
settle), driven visibly by their gears.

**Movement:** continuous forward flight through door after door; the doors ahead
recede in perspective, and the next one is always beginning to unlock as we
approach. The music sets the flight speed (smoothly).

**Music:** kicks trip one mechanism at a time (a latch releasing, a bolt
retracting, a gear advancing a tooth) so the beat is visible in one place;
the snare vents a jet of steam from one side; the bass sets flight and gear
speed; hats strike sparks or glints on rivets; the drop makes the doors more
elaborate, multi-stage and faster, perhaps inverting to white-on-black.
