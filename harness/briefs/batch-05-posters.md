# Batch 05 · Posters

Raph (2026-09-28): "I want to see a series of 'graphic design posters come to
life'." Twenty-two styles, his list, in his order.

## Series rules

- **It is a poster.** The frame is a printed poster (or cover, sign, zine
  page) that fills the stage, composed like the real thing in that movement:
  its grid or lack of one, its type, its palette, its printing process (offset,
  letterpress, screen print, photocopy, lithograph) with that process's
  texture and imperfections. Then it comes alive: forms slide, rotate,
  assemble, re-set, letters march, colours overprint.
- **Type matters.** Use the typefaces loaded by `web/fonts.js` (see its family
  list; check `VIZ_FONTS.has(family)` before relying on one) or draw letterforms
  as shapes. Wording is original and about music, sound, light, night, dancing:
  never real slogans, trademarks, logos or mastheads, and never a copy of a
  specific existing poster. Evoke the movement, don't reproduce its works.
- **Movements with real-world weight.** For Soviet propaganda, borrow the
  graphic language only (red and black, diagonals, photomontage, heroic
  scale); no state or political symbols. For ACT UP, honour the visual
  language of that activism (stark bold type, black ground, direct address,
  hot accent colour) with original words about community and the dance floor;
  do not use its specific symbols or slogans. For the New Yorker cover, invent
  your own masthead.
- **Taste.** No rainbow or glow unless the style itself calls for it (the neon
  sign and orphism do; most don't). Beat visible but confined; the drop
  obviously bigger (more elements in play, a bolder layout, a second colour);
  motion where the design allows. See `harness/TASTE.md`.

## The styles

- **01 · De Stijl (`destijl`).** Mondrian and van Doesburg: black grid lines,
  primary rectangles on white; the grid re-divides; kicks fill one cell with
  colour; the drop tilts the grid 45° (van Doesburg's counter-composition).
- **02 · Constructivist (`constructivist`).** Rodchenko and Lissitzky: red,
  black and cream; diagonal bars and wedges, a circle, bold sans type
  running at angles, photomontage-like cut shapes; forms fly in and lock on
  beats.
- **03 · Grunge typography (`grunge`).** David Carson and Ray Gun: layered,
  distressed, overlapping type at clashing sizes, cropped letters, scanned
  textures, tight leading gone wrong, legible only in part. Words slide and
  collide.
- **04 · Cubist (`cubist`).** Braque and Picasso's analytic cubism as a
  poster: a guitar, a bottle and a newspaper fragment broken into faceted
  planes in ochre, grey and brown, with stencilled letters; the facets shift
  and rotate with the music.
- **05 · Op art (`opart`).** A Vasarely or Riley poster: black and white
  (plus one colour allowed), checkerboards and grids that bulge into spheres,
  wave and warp. Crisp, no aliasing. Distinct from our Interference scene.
- **06 · Vorticism (`vorticism`).** Wyndham Lewis and BLAST: hard-edged
  angular machine forms, a vortex of shards, huge bold slab type in black and
  pink or puce; aggressive diagonals; shards whirl into the vortex on beats.
- **07 · Suprematism (`suprematism`).** Malevich: pure geometric forms (black
  square, red square, bars, circles) floating on white, weightless, slowly
  drifting and rotating in space; the drop brings in a flight of forms.
- **08 · Orphism / simultanism (`orphism`).** Sonia and Robert Delaunay:
  concentric discs and arcs of contrasting colour side by side, simultaneous
  contrast, rhythm; the discs turn like the music. The one place a full
  palette belongs, but painted and matte, not glowing.
- **09 · Penny dreadful (`pennydreadful`).** A Victorian serial cover: lurid
  woodcut illustration of a gothic scene (a moonlit street, a phantom), wild
  mixed wood-type headlines, a price in pence, cheap newsprint with ink spread;
  the illustration animates like a toy theatre.
- **10 · Letterpress stationery (`letterpress`).** Wood and metal type set in a
  chase and printed: deep impression into thick cotton paper, slight ink
  squeeze, misregistration between two colours, ornaments and rules; type sets
  itself line by line, the platen presses on the beat.
- **11 · Punk photocopy zine (`zine`).** Black-and-white photocopy: ransom-note
  cut-out letters, torn paper, tape, safety pins, high-contrast halftone
  photos, toner streaks and copier noise; pages pasted up and re-copied.
- **12 · Neon sign / diner Americana (`diner`).** A roadside diner at night:
  a neon sign with script and block letters, a tubing arrow that chases, a
  flickering letter, stars and a boomerang, reflected in chrome and a wet
  car park. The one place glow belongs.
- **13 · Zagreb school animation (`zagreb`).** The Zagreb Film style of the
  1960s: flat limited animation, bold stylised characters and backgrounds,
  witty geometric reduction, few colours on textured paper; a little story
  loop that dances.
- **14 · Italian futurist typography (`futurist`).** Marinetti's parole in
  libertà and Depero: words exploding across the page at every size and angle,
  onomatopoeia, speed lines, typographic noise that literally makes the sound
  of the music.
- **15 · WPA / New Deal poster (`wpa`).** Silkscreened National Parks and
  public-works posters: flat layered colour, a landscape (mountains, a lake,
  a lodge, a sky), a bold simple headline, visible screen-print layers slightly
  out of register; the scene lives (clouds, water, birds).
- **16 · Soviet propaganda (`soviet`).** The graphic language only (see series
  rules): red, black and cream, dramatic low angles, radiating beams, massed
  figures in silhouette, huge slanted type with original words about music
  and dancing together.
- **17 · ACT UP (`actup`).** See series rules: stark white or hot-pink bold
  type on black, direct address, collective urgency, with original words about
  community, bodies, the dance floor and care. Respectful and powerful.
- **18 · New Yorker cover (`newyorker`).** A painted magazine cover with an
  invented masthead: a gentle, witty illustrated city scene in gouache (a
  rooftop party, a record shop, a DJ on a fire escape), with seasonal details;
  the scene quietly animates.
- **19 · Glitch art (`glitch`).** A poster being corrupted: datamoshing,
  pixel sorting, channel shifts, JPEG block artefacts, scanline tears. The
  glitches follow the music; the underlying poster keeps trying to reassert
  itself. Keep the palette controlled.
- **20 · Low-poly 3D (`lowpoly`).** A late-90s/2010s low-poly poster: a
  flat-shaded faceted landscape or creature with gradient sky and bold type,
  gently turning; facets catch the light on beats.
- **21 · Liquid glass (`liquidglass`).** The 2025 "liquid glass" interface look
  as a poster: thick translucent glass lozenges and type that refract and
  magnify what is behind them, with specular edges and soft shadows, moving
  like liquid over a bold graphic background.
- **22 · The Designers Republic (`tdr`).** The Designers Republic's techno-
  consumerist style: dense Japanese-and-English sloganeering, fake corporate
  marks you invent, tiny data type, pictograms, a cool strict grid in acid and
  grey; everything scrolls and updates like a machine display.
